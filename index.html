How to use this Quick Reference Handbook

This guide is designed for high acuity, low occurrence skills happening in the ED. These include those that may be done by other specialties (e.g. obstetrics and resus hysterotomy, anaesthetics and subclavian line insertion), although all of them potentially fall under the purview and skillset of senior Emergency Medicine Physicians.

1. Find the procedure

Locate the procedure in the contents page. Navigate to the procedure spread.

2. Left Page: Checklist

On the left hand side, there is a full detailed checklist including precise equipment lists. Use this for thorough preparation.

3. Right Page: Flowchart

On the right side is the abbreviated flowchart. This is designed to be used in a similar manner to the ALS algorithms for real-time guidance.

Emergency Contacts

  • Consultant on call: Via switchboard
  • Nurse in charge: Resus
  • Theatre coordinator: #

1. Chest Drain Insertion

Indication: Traumatic haemothorax/pneumothorax with respiratory distress OR Tension pneumothorax if needle decompression failed.

Equipment Required

  • 28-32Fr chest drain (adult trauma)
  • Surgical prep kit (Chlorhexidine)
  • Local anaesthetic (1% lidocaine 20mL)
  • Scalpel (size 10 blade)
  • Spencer Wells forceps (blunt dissection)
  • Mattress suture (1-0 silk)
  • Sterile gloves & Gown
  • Underwater seal system (-20cmH₂O)

1. Call for Help & Assessment

Inform Consultant on call & Nurse in charge
Request surgical support if massive haemothorax anticipated
Allocate roles: Team Leader, Scribe, Procedure Clinician
Confirm Indication: Consider USS or portable CXR if patient is stable enough

2. Preparation

Obtain consent if possible (explain risks: pain, bleeding, infection, visceral injury)
Position: 30-45° sitting up (if C-spine allows), arm abducted 90°, hand behind head
Identify Landmarks (Triangle of Safety): Ant. edge Lat Dorsi, Lat. edge Pec Major, Apex of Axilla, 5th ICS
Assistant to hold breast tissue out of the way if required

3. Procedure

Sterile prep and drape. Strict aseptic technique.
Infiltrate 1% Lidocaine (up to 20mL) - Skin, Subcutaneous, Periosteum, Pleura
Incision: 2-3cm transverse, just **superior** to the rib border (avoid N/V bundle)
Blunt dissection with Spencer Wells forceps through intercostal muscles
Digital Exploration: Insert finger to confirm pleural space & sweep for adhesions
CRITICAL: NEVER USE A TROCAR
Insert drain guided by finger: Direct Posteriorly and Superiorly
Ensure all drainage holes are within the pleural cavity

4. Completion

Connect to underwater seal (ensure water level correct)
Confirm swing with respiration and bubbling
Secure with **Mattress Suture** (1-0 Silk). Do not purse-string.
Apply sterile dressing (e.g., drain fix + tegaderm)
Post-Procedure: Urgent CXR. Monitor output hourly.
Surgical Review Criteria: >1500mL initial output OR >200mL/hr for 2-4 hours

2. Emergency Thoracotomy

Indication: Traumatic cardiac arrest. Penetrating chest < 10 mins OR Blunt chest < 5 mins.

Equipment Required (Thoracotomy Tray)

  • Scalpel (Size 10 and 20 blades)
  • Rib spreaders (Finochietto)
  • Heavy scissors (Mayo/Tuff Cut)
  • Gigli saw (for sternal extension)
  • Toothed forceps (Bonney's)
  • Large bore suction (Yankauer x2)
  • Aortic Clamp
  • 3-0 Prolene (cardiac repair) / Skin Stapler
  • Internal Defibrillator paddles (if available)

1. Immediate Activation

Call Consultant & NIC
Call Cardiothoracic Registrar IMMEDIATELY
Activate Major Haemorrhage Protocol (Pack A)
Contraindication: Asystole > 10 minutes

2. Preparation

Continue CPR (unless interfering with procedure)
Intubate and ventilate with 100% O2 (disconnect briefly during incision if needed)
Rapid skin prep (Chlorhexidine/Betadine) - time permitting
Right arm abducted to 90 degrees

3. Procedure: Left Anterolateral Thoracotomy

Incision: 4th or 5th ICS (Nipple level in male), from Mid-axillary line to sternal border
Single bold incision with large blade through skin and subcutaneous tissue
Stop ventilation briefly. Cut through intercostal muscles with heavy scissors/scalpel.
Insert rib spreaders (handle down toward bed) and crank open vigorously.

4. HOT Protocol

H: Haemorrhage Control - Twist lung hilum to control lung bleed. Compress aorta against spine or apply aortic clamp.
O: Open Pericardium - Use toothed forceps to lift pericardium. Make longitudinal incision anterior & parallel to Phrenic Nerve.
T: Treat Heart - Evacuate clot ('birth' the heart). Identify wound.
Control cardiac wound: Digital pressure, Foley catheter balloon, Skin staples, or Sutures (3-0 Prolene).
Perform internal cardiac massage (two hands/flat palms) if no output.
Consider Clamshell: If right sided injury suspected or poor access, extend incision across sternum.

3. Escharotomy

Indication: Circumferential full-thickness burns with compartment syndrome (Limb: pain/pallor/pulselessness; Chest: reduced expansion/high vent pressures).

Equipment Required

  • Scalpel (Size 10 or 22 blade)
  • Electrocautery (Diathermy) - Preferred if available
  • Sterile drapes and gloves
  • Marking pen
  • Dressings (Alginate/Kaltostat)
  • Ketamine (for procedural sedation/analgesia)

1. Call for Help

Consultant + NIC + Burns Centre Advice
Alert Theatre (Fasciotomy may be required if deep muscle involvement)

2. Preparation

Position patient to expose full area (supine, limbs abducted)
Mark incision lines with pen (Mid-axial lines)
Anaesthesia: Full thickness burns are insensate, but patient will likely require Ketamine sedation for distress and incomplete adjacent burns.
TIMING CRITICAL: PERFORM WITHIN 2 HOURS

3. Procedure: Limb Escharotomy

Incisions: Make BOTH Medial AND Lateral incisions along the mid-axial lines.
Extend from proximal to distal, crossing involved joints (risk areas).
Depth: Cut through eschar only until subcutaneous fat bulges. Avoid deep fascia.
Hands: Incise along radial side of thumb and ulnar side of little finger. Avoid web spaces.
Feet: Medial and lateral aspects.

4. Procedure: Chest Escharotomy

Incise along bilateral anterior axillary lines (Clavicle to costal margin).
Join with transverse incision (costal margin) if needed ('Roman Breastplate').
Aim: To allow chest wall excursion.

5. Post-Procedure

Apply haemostatic dressings (e.g. Kaltostat). Elevate limbs.
Reassess pulses (Doppler) and airway pressures.

4. Landmark Subclavian Line

Indication: Need for central access where ultrasound is unavailable or failed.

Equipment Required

  • Central line kit (Triple Lumen)
  • Sterile Gown, Gloves, Cap, Large drape
  • Chlorhexidine prep
  • 1% Lidocaine (10mL) + needles (25G/21G)
  • 10mL Syringe (for aspiration)
  • Guidewire, Dilator, Scalpel (11)
  • Suture (Silk/Prolene) & Dressing (Tegaderm)
  • Saline flushes x3

1. Call for Help

Consultant + NIC
Request senior support if inexperienced (Anaesthetics/ITU)

2. Preparation

Position: Supine, 15° Trendelenburg (head down) to distend veins.
Arm adducted by side. Head turned to contralateral side.
Towel roll longitudinally between scapulae (optional - to open clavicle space).
Full sterile technique. Pre-flush all lumens and clamp lines.

3. Procedure

Landmark: Identify junction of middle and medial third of clavicle.
Infiltrate LA at insertion site (1-2cm inferior to clavicle).
Insert introducer needle on 10mL syringe (bevel up).
Place index finger in suprasternal notch.
Advance needle under clavicle, aiming towards suprasternal notch.
Keep needle parallel to floor. Aspirate continuously.
Flashback: Dark, non-pulsatile venous blood.
Remove syringe (OCCOLUDE HUB TO PREVENT AIR EMBOLISM).
Seldinger technique: Insert Guidewire (should pass easily) -> Remove needle -> Incise skin -> Dilate -> Rail road Catheter.

4. Post-Procedure

Aspirate and flush all lumens to confirm flow.
Secure line with suture and sterile dressing.
URGENT CXR: Check tip position (SVC/RA junction) & exclude pneumothorax/haemothorax.

5. Lateral Canthotomy

Indication: Orbital Compartment Syndrome (IOP >40mmHg OR Proptosis + RAPD + Vision Loss). Time Critical < 2 hours.

Equipment Required

  • 1-2% Lidocaine with adrenaline (2mL)
  • Small syringe + 25G/27G needle
  • Small straight scissors (Iris or Tenotomy)
  • Toothed forceps (Adson or similar)
  • Haemostat / Arterial clamp (Mosquito)
  • Sterile gauze & saline
  • Irrigation fluid (if chemical injury)

1. Immediate Action

Call Consultant + NIC
Call Ophthalmology URGENTLY (Do not delay procedure if signs present)
Assess Visual Acuity and IOP if possible.
Procedure is SIGHT SAVING. Do not wait for CT.

2. Procedure Steps

Clean lateral canthus with saline/iodine.
Inject 1-2mL LA into lateral canthus (directing needle away from globe).
**Crimp:** Apply haemostat to lateral canthus (1cm) for 30-60s to devascularise.
Step 1: Canthotomy - Use scissors to cut full thickness through the crimped lateral canthus horizontally (~1cm) towards orbital rim.
Step 2: Cantholysis (ESSENTIAL) - Retract lower lid. Identify inferior crus of lateral canthal tendon (feels like a guitar string).
Point scissors inferoposteriorly towards lateral rim. Cut the inferior crus.
Check: Lower lid should be freely mobile and pull away from the globe.
If inadequate decompression: Cut superior crus (Superior Cantholysis).

3. Post-Procedure

Recheck IOP (<40mmHg) and Vision/RAPD.
Apply antibiotic ointment and sterile dressing.
Do not bandage tightly. Monitor for re-accumulation.
Arrange urgent CT Orbits and Ophthalmology review.

6. Major Haemorrhage Control

Indication: Life-threatening haemorrhage.

Equipment Required

  • Rapid Infuser (Belmont/Level 1)
  • Fluid Warmer
  • Major Haemorrhage Pack (Porter to Blood Bank)
  • Large bore IV cannulas (14G/16G) or IO Drill
  • Pelvic Binder
  • Tourniquets / Haemostatic Gauze (Celox)
  • Tranexamic Acid (1g) + Calcium Chloride

1. Activation

ACTIVATE MAJOR HAEMORRHAGE PROTOCOL via Switchboard (2222).
State location (ED Resus) and estimated blood loss.
Alert Theatre / Interventional Radiology / Porter.
Allocate Scribe to document blood products.

2. Immediate Control

Apply direct pressure to bleeding point.
Limb: Apply tourniquet proximal to wound if uncontrolled.
Pelvis: Apply pelvic binder over greater trochanters.
Wound: Pack with haemostatic gauze.

3. Resuscitation

Insert 2x Large Bore IV (14G/16G) or Humeral IO.
Take samples: FBC, U&E, Coagulation, Fibrinogen, X-match.
Tranexamic Acid: 1g IV bolus (within 3h of injury) + 1g infusion.
Blood Products: Give Pack A (4 units O-Neg/O-Pos RBC + 4 FFP).
Ratio 1:1:1 (RBC : FFP : Platelets). Avoid crystalloids.
Permissive Hypotension: Target SBP 80-90mmHg (radial pulse).
*Head Injury Target: SBP >110mmHg to maintain perfusion.

4. Monitoring

VBG every 15-30 mins.
Targets: Hb >70, pH >7.2, Lactate <4, Temp >36°C, Ca >1.1, Fibrinogen >1.5.
Administer 10mL 10% Calcium Chloride IV for every 4 units blood.
Prevent Hypothermia (Bair Hugger, Warm Fluids).

7. MaxFax Trauma

Indication: Severe maxillofacial trauma with uncontrolled bleeding.

Equipment Required

  • Good lighting (Headlight)
  • Suction (Yankauer x2)
  • Magill forceps
  • Thudichum / Killian speculum
  • Rapid Rhino (7.5cm/9cm) or Merocel packs
  • Foley Catheter (12-14Fr) for posterior packing
  • Umbilical clamp / padding
  • Tranexamic Acid 5% soak / Adrenaline 1:1000

1. Call for Help

Consultant + NIC.
Anaesthetics (Likely difficult airway due to blood/anatomy).
MaxFax / ENT URGENTLY.

2. Airway & Assessment

Sit patient upright & lean forward (if C-spine/conscious state allows).
High flow oxygen (15L NRB) + Continuous Suction.
Assess Source: Anterior nasal? Posterior nasal? Oral/Tongue?
Examine for loose teeth/bone fragments (aspiration risk).

3. Control Steps

Oral Bleed: Direct pressure with gauze/adrenaline soak. Suture if visible vessel.
Anterior Epistaxis: Suction clots. Insert Rapid Rhino (soak in water) or Merocel. Inflate pilot cuff.
Posterior Epistaxis (if anterior fails): Use Foley Catheter Technique.
Lubricate Foley (12-14Fr). Insert into nasopharynx (until tip seen in mouth).
Inflate balloon with 10-15mL water.
Retract catheter anteriorly until it lodges against posterior choana.
While maintaining traction, pack anterior nose with ribbon gauze/Merocel.
Secure catheter at nostril with umbilical clamp (padded with gauze to prevent necrosis).

4. Adjuncts

IV Tranexamic Acid 1g.
Correct coagulopathy.
DO NOT REMOVE POSTERIOR PACKS IN ED. Risk of torrential re-bleed. Specialist Procedure.

8. Resuscitative Hysterotomy

Indication: Maternal cardiac arrest (Pregnancy ≥20 weeks). No ROSC after 4 minutes.

Equipment Required

  • Scalpel (Size 10 and 20 blades)
  • Heavy scissors (Mayo)
  • Umbilical Cord Clamps x2
  • Large abdominal swabs
  • Suction
  • Neonatal Resusitaire (warm & checked)
  • Forceps/Clamps (Spencer Wells/Kochers)

1. Immediate Activation

Call Consultant + NIC.
Obstetric Registrar IMMEDIATELY.
Neonatal Team (if viable ≥24 weeks).
Allocate Timekeeper (critical).

2. Indication Check

Gestation: Fundus at/above Umbilicus (Approx ≥20 weeks).
Arrest: No ROSC at 4 minutes of ALS.
PERFORM BY 5 MINUTES FROM ARREST
Goal: Primary aim is Maternal Resuscitation (Relieves aortocaval compression).

3. Procedure

Continue CPR & Manual Left Uterine Displacement throughout.
Splash & Dash: Rapid betadine pour (no sterile drapes needed).
Incision 1: Large vertical midline incision from Xiphisternum/Umbilicus to Pubis.
Cut deep through all layers (skin, fat, rectus sheath) to peritoneum.
Retract bladder downwards (if visible/time allows).
Incision 2: Small vertical incision in lower uterine segment. Insert fingers to lift wall away from fetus.
Extend uterine incision vertically with heavy scissors (guarding fetus with fingers).
Deliver baby, clamp cord x2 & cut. Hand to Neonatal Team.

4. Post-Delivery

Continue Maternal CPR (Effectiveness often improves immediately).
Deliver placenta if spontaneously separating. Otherwise leave.
Administer Oxytocin 10 units IV/IM. Pack uterus with wet swabs.
Apply clamps to bleeding uterine edges. Transfer to Theatre.

9. Open Thoracostomy

Indication: Massive haemothorax / Tension Pneumothorax (Failed needle) / Traumatic Arrest.

Equipment Required

  • Scalpel (Size 10 blade)
  • Spencer Wells / Kelly forceps
  • Antiseptic solution (Chlorhexidine)
  • Sterile gloves
  • Chest Drain (28-32Fr) - if ROSC achieved

1. Call for Help

Consultant + NIC.
Cardiothoracic Surgery if massive haemothorax.
Anaesthetics/Airway support.

2. Preparation

Position: Supine or 30°, Arm abducted 90° (if possible).
Landmarks: Triangle of Safety (5th ICS, Ant. to Mid-axillary line).
Rapid skin prep.

3. Procedure: Finger Thoracostomy

Make 4-6cm Transverse Incision (large enough for finger) just superior to rib border.
Blunt dissection with forceps down to pleura.
Puncture pleura with forceps tip (spread open).
Insert Finger: Push finger through pleura (Expect rush of air/blood).
Digital Sweep: Sweep finger 360° to confirm pleural space, feel lung expansion, and clear adhesions/clots.
Leave open (simple thoracostomy) in arrest to allow decompression.

4. Drain Insertion (If indicated/ROSC)

If output obtained or ROSC achieved, insert large bore drain (28-32Fr).
Guide drain posteriorly & superiorly using finger as guide.
NEVER USE TROCAR
Connect to underwater seal. Secure with suture.

10. Pericardiocentesis (Echo-Guided)

Indication: Cardiac Tamponade (Obstructive shock). Symptomatic moderate-large effusion non-responsive to medical therapy.

Equipment Required (ESC Guidance)

  • Echocardiography Machine + Cardiac Probe
  • Sterile probe cover & sterile gel
  • 16-18G Teflon-sheathed needle
  • J-tip guidewire & Dilator (6-8Fr)
  • Pigtail catheter (6-8Fr)
  • 3-way stopcock & 20ml Syringe
  • 1% Lidocaine
  • ECG Monitor & Resus Trolley

1. Call for Help

Consultant + NIC.
Cardiology / Cardiothoracic Registrar.
Alert Theatres (in case of myocardial puncture/fail).
Check Coagulation (unless cardiac arrest/peri-arrest).

2. Preparation & Echo

Position: Semi-reclining (30-45°) to pool fluid inferiorly/anteriorly.
Continuous ECG & BP Monitoring.
Echo Assessment: Identify largest fluid pocket and closest distance from skin.
Choose Approach:
- Apical: 1-2cm lateral to apex beat (5th-7th ICS). Often best for loculated fluid.
- Subcostal: Between xiphisternum and left costal margin (Angle 15-30° to L shoulder).
- Parasternal: 5th Left ICS close to sternal margin.

3. Procedure

Sterile prep, drape, and LA infiltration.
Advance needle while aspirating, following visualized trajectory.
Fluid Aspirated: Confirm non-clotting pericardial fluid.
Confirmation: Inject agitated saline (bubbles seen in pericardium on Echo).
Seldinger Technique: Insert wire -> Dilate -> Insert Pigtail Catheter.
Aspirate fluid until haemodynamics improve.

4. Post-Procedure

Secure drain and flush with heparinised saline if available.
CXR: Check for Pneumothorax.
Monitor for 'Pericardial Decompression Syndrome' (Pulm Oedema/Shock).
Avoid Aortic Dissection cases if possible (Surgical management preferred).
